## The AI PM Challenge

Traditional PM frameworks were built for deterministic software. AI products are probabilistic — they don't always give the same answer, they can hallucinate, and "quality" is fuzzy. Here's how to adapt proven frameworks for the AI era.

## Prioritization: LNO for AI Features

Shreyas Doshi's LNO framework (Leverage, Neutral, Overhead) maps perfectly to AI feature decisions:

- **Leverage tasks:** Improving model quality, building evaluation frameworks, designing agent architectures — these compound in value.
- **Neutral tasks:** Adding new prompt templates, basic UI tweaks — necessary but not transformative.
- **Overhead tasks:** Manual data labeling, one-off model comparisons — minimize or automate these.

**AI-specific insight:** In AI products, evaluation infrastructure is almost always a Leverage task. Teams that invest in evals early ship better products faster.

## Strategy: DHM Model for AI Products

Gibson Biddle's DHM (Delight, Hard-to-copy, Margin-enhancing) framework helps assess AI feature strategy:

- **Delight:** Does the AI feature create genuine user delight? Not just "cool AI" but actually solving a pain point.
- **Hard-to-copy:** Proprietary data, fine-tuned models, and unique agent architectures are moats. Raw LLM wrappers are not.
- **Margin-enhancing:** AI can improve margins (automation) or destroy them (expensive inference). Model your unit economics.

## Product-Market Fit: The Sean Ellis Test for AI

Ask users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [AI feature]?"

**The twist for AI:** Users often overvalue AI features in surveys because they sound impressive. Complement the Sean Ellis test with **behavioral data** — are users actually using the AI features repeatedly, or just trying them once?

## Key Takeaways

1. **Don't reinvent frameworks** — adapt proven ones with AI-specific lenses
2. **Evaluation is your superpower** — it's the AI equivalent of analytics
3. **Measure behavior, not sentiment** — users say they want AI; watch if they use it
4. **Cost is a first-class product concern** — unlike traditional software, AI features have meaningful marginal costs
